# 🌀 Suction Feed

> [!abstract] Overview
> When main tank fuel pump pressure is low, each engine can draw fuel from its corresponding main tank through a line that bypasses the pumps entirely. Dissolved air released during climb can restrict this flow.

## 📋 Quick Facts

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Low main fuel pump pressure |
| Path | Line bypassing the main tank pumps |
| Risk mechanism | Climb → decreasing air pressure → dissolved air releases from fuel → collects in suction line → restricts flow |
| Consequence | Thrust deterioration or engine flameout at high altitude |
| Flameout may occur as low as | **13,000 ft** during a high rate of climb |
| Air depletion | Depends on altitude, fuel temperature, fuel type |
| After dissolved air depletes | Engine may support suction feed at cruise power |
| Dual use | Main tank bypass valves may also be used for **suction defueling** |

> [!warning] If Fuel Flow Reduces During Climb
> Fuel flow should stabilise within a few minutes. **Maintain a low rate of climb** and **action the QRH**.
