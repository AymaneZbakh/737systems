- Bleed air is supplied from **Engines** , **APU** or **external air cart** 

## ENGINE BLEED AIR
- Engine bleed air is obtained from the [[5th and 9th stages of the compressor]] section
- When 5th stage low pressure bleed air is insufficient for the bleed air system requirements, the high stage valve modulates open to maintain adequate bleed air pressure. 
- During takeoff, climb, and most cruise conditions, low pressure bleed air from the 5th stage is adequate and the high stage valve remainb s closed.
- It is transferred from sources to users by **bleed air duct** , they need to withstand high temperatures and prevent heat transfer
---
- Bleed air system is divided into Left and Right sides by [[Isolation Valve]]
- All  bleed air valves are butterfly type ; their main advantage is they can open and close quickly
- One engine should operate only one pack at a time
---
- When using external pneumatic source  , supplied bleed air should not exceed **60 psi** and temperature **232°C**
---
- At sea level with engine thrust between *27% and 47% N1* the bleed air is regulated by High stage valve (9th stage) with duct pressure at *32 psi +/- 6psi*
- Between approximately *47% and 54% N1* , pressure source switches over from 9th stage to 5th stage ; during the switchover and below 27% N1 the pressure is not regulated to any specific value
- At sea level with engine thrust *above 54% N1* the bleed air is regulated by [[PRSOV]] (5th stage) with duct pressure at *42 psi +/- 8psi*
- Duct pressure above **50 psi** (when engine is bleed air source) is considered high and requires maintenance action
---
---
## APU BLEED AIR VALVE:

- Permits APU bleed air to flow to the bleed air duct
- Closes automatically when APU is shut down
- DC controlled and pressure aoperated
- With both the APU and engine bleed air valves open , and the engines operating at idle thrust, there is a possibility of APU bleed air back-pressuring the 9th stage modulating and shutoff valve. This would cause the 9th stage valve to close

---
---
## GROUND PNEUMATIC CONNECTOR
- Allows engine to be started with APU INOP
- Connects to the right side of bleed manifold
- Do not mistake with Ground Conditioned Air Connector
- External pneumatic source must control the temperature and pressure within placard limits (*232°C and 60 psi*)
- Battery power shall be ON and PACK switches OFF to ensure that pack valves will be closed during ground start

---
---
## DUCT PRESSURE TRANSMITTER
- Each side of the bleed air manifold has its own duct pressure transmitter, which sends pressure indications to the air conditioning panel
- When doing engine cross-bleed start, the thrust on the operating engine needs to be increased to obtain duct pressure of *30 psi*. This will ensure sufficient pressure for the air starter of the other engine
- Duct pressure **above 50 psi** is considered high and maintenance action is required
- When using an external pneumatic source , supplied bleed air should not exceed placard limit of *60 psi*

**DUCT PRESSURE SPLIT**
The pressure difference between the left and right ducts
- there is no control system to regulate both ducts to a common pressure
- When the isolation valve is closed, the left and right ducts operate independently. 
- To determine whether the duct pressure is within limits it has to be compared to the engine N1 and airplane altitude. This why the maintenance will ask you to fill in a bleed sheet (ALT,N1,duct pressure), then they will compare the data with "duct pressure versus N1 " graphs at different altitudes
- Typically , with no system faults, the duct pressure split is caused by a crossover from low to high stage occurring at a different time for the left and right sides
- The crossover is slightly different when regarding the thrust
- If it can be determined that the duct pressure split is due to the crossover no further maintenance action is required
- The duct pressure during Take off, climb and cruise should be above 18 psi and a 10 psi after the top of descent. Duct pressure below those limits requires maintenance action

### PRECOOLER AND PRECOOLER CONTROL VALVE

- Before the hot bleed air enters the duct, it is necessary to decrease its temperature by precooler to avoid damage
- Precooler is an air-to-air heat exchanger which is cooled by regulated fan air supply
- Precooler control valve controls the amount of air the heat exchanger gets for cooling to keep the bleed air temperature within 199°C and 229°C
- If the precooler cannot cool the bleed air enough, the PRSOV closes and *BLEED TRIP OFF* illuminates
- When on the ground with the wing anti-ice ON the precooler control valve fully opens to cool the bleed air entering the ducts as much as possible. this is to prevent structural overheat of the leading edges of the wing when there is insufficient cooling from the outside airflow

### NITROGEN GENERATION SYSTEM

- It is part of the aircraft's fuel tank inerting system (Inert : not capable of ignition/explosion under normal conditions)
- Reduces the risk of fuel tank explosions by reducing oxygen concentration inside fuel tanks.<mark class="hltr-g"></mark>
- The air passes through a *membrane* filter in the air separation module
- The membrane separates oxygen and nitrogen
- The oxygen enriched air is vented overboard
- The nitrogen enriched air is retained and is routed to the center fuel tank

### BLEED INDICATIONS (overview)

*DUAL BLEED*
- Triggered by bleed switch position and isolation valve position
- Illuminates when APU bleed air valve is OPEN and:
	- ENG 1 bleed air switch ON or
	- ENG 2 bleed air switch ON with isolation valve OPEN
- Operate the engines at IDLE thrust only (Engine bleed could otherwise backpressure the APU and cause damage)
*BLEED TRIP OFF*
- Over temperature (254°C) or over pressure (220 psi)
- **PRSOV automatically closes** to prevent damage to the duct
- To reset the Bleed Trip Off, the conditions have to return below the limit, and [[TRIP RESET switch]] has to be pushed
- Can happen especially after no engine bleed take-off with high engine thrust 
*WING BODY OVERHEAT*
- If the bleed air duct starts leaking hot bleed air, it could damage airplane components and structure. The area around the ducts is monitored, and if an overheat condition is sensed *WING BODY OVERHEAT* will illuminate
- Needs 115V AC power for system to detect overheat condition
- Watch out for subsequent A/C or Pressure failures on the **opposite side**
- Do not use **Wing Anti-Ice**
